# Monetization Brainstorm

## Framing

Piarco Live can become more than a flight tracker. Inside Trinidad and Tobago, the strongest commercial angle is a real-time movement intelligence product around aviation, ports, tourism, logistics, and visitor experience.

The current prototype combines:

- passenger flight visibility
- airport board style data
- local geography
- weather/METAR context
- ship and port movement context
- Trinidad/Tobago-specific routes and ports

That creates several possible business models. The best path is probably not consumer subscriptions first. Trinidad is a small market, so the stronger early monetization path is B2B, sponsorship, airport/port partnerships, and operational dashboards.

## Core Value Proposition

Piarco Live helps businesses and agencies answer:

- When are people arriving?
- When are aircraft delayed?
- What flights are currently inbound or outbound?
- What cargo, ferry, vessel, or port movement may affect operations?
- What weather conditions may affect movement?
- Where should staff, vehicles, pickups, rooms, crews, or logistics resources be positioned?

The consumer-facing flight tracker can be the public surface. The business product can be the paid layer.

## Business Models

### Model A: Sponsored Public Tracker

Free public site funded by sponsorship.

Revenue:

- monthly sponsor placement
- branded weather strip
- sponsored arrivals page
- sponsored travel tips
- seasonal campaign placements

Possible sponsors:

- telecoms
- banks
- travel insurance companies
- hotels
- car rentals
- tourism campaigns
- airport food/retail brands

Pros:

- easiest consumer adoption
- good for brand awareness
- public utility angle

Cons:

- ad sales can be slow
- sponsorship revenue may be inconsistent
- needs meaningful traffic

### Model B: B2B Dashboard Subscription

Paid operational dashboards for businesses.

Revenue:

- monthly SaaS subscription
- tiered by number of users, locations, or alert volume

Possible tiers:

- Starter: watched flights, alerts, public board
- Pro: team dashboard, history, exports, WhatsApp/email alerts
- Enterprise: API access, custom branding, custom data integrations, SLA

Likely buyers:

- hotels
- ground handlers
- transfer companies
- logistics firms
- vessel agents
- port service companies

Pros:

- recurring revenue
- clear operational value
- does not require huge public traffic

Cons:

- requires sales and onboarding
- customers will expect reliability
- real data licensing may become expensive

### Model C: White-Label Tracker

Sell branded versions to organizations.

Revenue:

- setup fee
- monthly hosting/support fee
- custom feature fees

Examples:

- hotel-branded guest arrival board
- logistics company-branded cargo movement page
- port agency-branded vessel status page
- tourism-branded travel status page

Pros:

- high perceived value
- easier to sell as a finished product
- can reuse the same core platform

Cons:

- too much customization can become agency work
- needs strict template boundaries

### Model D: Alerts-As-A-Service

Sell notifications rather than the full dashboard.

Revenue:

- subscription per user/team
- per-alert or per-watchlist pricing
- WhatsApp/SMS markup

Alert types:

- flight delayed
- flight landed
- aircraft within X nautical miles
- departure airborne
- vessel approaching port
- ferry movement update
- weather threshold crossed

Pros:

- simple value proposition
- works well for hotels, taxis, transfers, and logistics
- mobile-first behavior fits Trinidad business workflows

Cons:

- WhatsApp/SMS delivery costs
- alert accuracy depends on data quality
- users may churn if alerts are noisy

### Model E: Data/API Licensing

Offer movement data through API or embeds.

Revenue:

- API subscription
- usage-based pricing
- widget licensing

Customers:

- local travel websites
- logistics platforms
- hotel systems
- media sites
- internal dashboards

Pros:

- scalable if data pipeline is strong
- can power partners without building custom UI for everyone

Cons:

- requires robust data infrastructure
- needs clear data licensing rights
- harder to sell early without proven demand

### Model F: Government Or Public-Sector Contract

Position the product as public travel infrastructure.

Possible buyers/partners:

- airport authority
- tourism bodies
- transport agencies
- digital government units
- emergency management stakeholders

Use cases:

- public travel information
- disruption dashboards
- visitor flow monitoring
- national transport visibility

Pros:

- potentially larger contracts
- strong public utility case

Cons:

- procurement cycles can be slow
- compliance and support expectations are higher
- political and administrative complexity

## Most Practical Starting Strategy

Start with a two-sided approach:

1. Public tracker for credibility and traffic.
2. Paid dashboards/alerts for businesses that lose money when arrivals, departures, or vessel movements are poorly timed.

Best first paid targets:

- airport transfer companies
- hotels near Port of Spain/Piarco
- ground handlers
- freight forwarders
- vessel agents
- port-adjacent logistics firms

These customers have an immediate operational reason to care.

## Pricing Hypotheses

These are starting hypotheses, not validated prices.

- Sponsored public placement: TT$2,500 to TT$10,000 per month depending on traffic and exclusivity.
- Small business dashboard: TT$500 to TT$1,500 per month.
- Pro operations dashboard: TT$2,000 to TT$6,000 per month.
- White-label setup: TT$10,000 to TT$50,000 one-time, plus monthly support.
- Enterprise/API package: custom pricing.

The strongest early signal is whether a business will pay for alerts that save staff time, vehicle waiting time, or customer-service calls.

## Validation Plan

1. Interview 5 airport transfer operators.
2. Interview 5 hotels or guest service managers.
3. Interview 5 freight/logistics companies.
4. Interview 3 vessel or port agents.
5. Ask each:
   - what do you currently use to track arrivals?
   - how often are you surprised by delays?
   - who watches this information?
   - what happens when the information is wrong?
   - would alerts or a dashboard save time or money?
   - what would you pay monthly if it worked?
6. Sell a manual concierge version before building full automation.

## Key Risks

- Real flight data licensing may be expensive.
- Free public flight data may be incomplete or rate-limited.
- ADS-B coverage around Trinidad may vary by provider.
- AIS/maritime data may require commercial licensing.
- Public users may not pay directly.
- B2B customers will expect accuracy and uptime.
- Too many custom dashboards could turn the product into consulting.

## Strongest Product Positioning

The most defensible positioning is:

> A Trinidad and Tobago movement intelligence dashboard for flights, ports, weather, and arrival operations.

That is broader than a consumer flight tracker, but still focused enough to sell locally.
